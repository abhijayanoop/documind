Compensation for machine learning engineers at CRED is structured by level, and this document lays out the bands in detail for internal hiring and calibration purposes.

Mid-level ML engineers, roughly two to five years of relevant experience, earn a base salary in the range of 25 to 35 LPA. This band applies to engineers who can independently own a model or pipeline end to end, covering data preparation, training, evaluation, and deployment, but who typically still work within an established system architecture rather than defining it. On top of the base, mid-level engineers receive ESOPs vesting over four years with a one-year cliff, and an annual performance bonus targeted at 10 to 15 percent of base salary, paid out based on a mix of individual and team performance ratings.

Senior ML engineers earn between 40 and 60 LPA. This band is reserved for engineers who define system architecture for their area, mentor mid-level engineers, and are expected to make judgment calls on build-versus-buy and model-versus-heuristic trade-offs without close supervision. Senior-level ESOP grants are proportionally larger and often include refresh grants after the first two years to retain top performers.

Staff and principal-level ML engineers, who typically operate across multiple teams or set technical direction for an entire domain such as fraud detection or credit risk modelling, are compensated on a separate, individually negotiated band that is reviewed case by case rather than published as a fixed range. These offers usually combine a higher cash component with a significantly larger ESOP grant, and are approved by a compensation committee rather than a single hiring manager.

These bands are reviewed annually, typically each April, and are benchmarked against comparable fintech companies to stay competitive. Actual offers within a band vary based on the candidate's depth of production experience — engineers who have shipped and operated RAG systems, agentic workflows, or model evaluation infrastructure in production, rather than only research or prototyping, are generally placed toward the top of their band. Internal transfers from other engineering disciplines into ML roles are placed based on demonstrated ML project impact rather than tenure at the company.

Location and team also factor into final offers within a band. Engineers on teams with a demonstrated, direct revenue or fraud-prevention impact are more likely to be placed at the top of their band than engineers on purely exploratory or research-oriented teams, all else being equal. Relocation candidates receive a one-time relocation allowance that is separate from and does not count against the base salary band.

Compensation conversations should be handled carefully: candidates should never be shown a specific band before an offer stage, and hiring managers should only discuss the 25 to 35 LPA mid-level range, or the corresponding senior range, with candidates who have already cleared the technical rounds and reached the offer discussion. Sharing exact band figures earlier in the process, even informally, is treated as a process violation because it can bias how candidates negotiate later. This document itself is restricted to hiring managers and above for that reason, and should never be forwarded to a candidate directly under any circumstances.

Beyond base salary and ESOPs, mid-level and senior ML engineers are eligible for the same benefits package as the rest of engineering, including comprehensive health insurance covering dependents, an annual learning and development stipend that can be used for conference attendance or paid courses, and a flexible work-from-home policy that allows up to three remote days per week outside of on-call weeks. Engineers on payments-critical teams that require in-person incident response receive an additional on-call allowance on top of base compensation, paid per rotation rather than as a flat monthly amount, to reflect the variable burden of on-call weeks.

Internal equity reviews happen twice a year, in April alongside the main band review and again in October as a smaller off-cycle check, specifically to catch cases where an engineer's scope has grown faster than their compensation band would suggest, such as an engineer who has taken on staff-level responsibilities while still nominally classified as senior. Managers are expected to proactively flag these cases rather than waiting for the engineer to raise it, and calibration committees look unfavorably on teams where equity corrections only ever happen in response to a competing offer, since that pattern erodes trust in the review process over time. Referral bonuses for successful ML engineering hires are handled through a separate program and are not deducted from the referring engineer's own compensation band in any way.
